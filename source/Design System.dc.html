01 / Color
A near-monochrome palette
Four neutrals do 98% of the work. Color is an event, not a surface — it appears in the wordmark and in status, nowhere else. No gradients, no tinted panels, no colored buttons.

02 / Type
One grotesk, two weights, two colors
A single neo-grotesk carries everything. Hierarchy is built from weight and color far more than from size — the signature move is a bold ink line with a same-size muted line under it.
Family
Helvetica Neue · Helvetica · Arial
Tight apertures, level terminals, no personality tics. Substitute any true neo-grotesk (Helvetica Now, Söhne, Inter Display at tight tracking) — never a geometric or humanist sans, which breaks the tone immediately. Weights in play: 400, 500, 600, 700. Optical tracking is negative on everything above 24px.

Do
Alternate ink and muted within a long statement to give a paragraph rhythm without adding color, size, or rules. Sentence case everywhere except micro-labels. Numerals and units live in the muted role.
Don't
No italics for emphasis, no all-caps headings, no letter-spacing on anything above 15px, no third typeface, no colored text outside the semantic red.

04 / Parts
Five components, no more
The reference site ships a deliberately small kit. Nothing has a shadow. Nothing has a fill except the primary button and the surface well. Radii are binary: pill for controls, 6–8px for blocks.

Pairing

States
Hover: ink→ink-hover, or border hairline→ink. Focus: 2px ink outline, 2px offset.
Restraint
One primary per section. Never a colored, ghost-on-image, or icon-only pill.
Content card — generic
A borderless, shadowless stack: an optional media well, then a fixed three-line text block — title (500/ink) · meta (muted) · detail (ink) — with an optional icon action floated to the title's right. The container is invisible; the text block is the component. Media is optional and can be dropped entirely for text-led domains, in which case the card becomes an indented list row.

05 / Tone
Why it feels trustworthy
The aesthetic argument is we don't need to sell you. Preserve that when the subject changes; it survives any content, and it is the only part of this system that can't be re-specified as a token.
Restraint as credibility
Nothing is decorated, so nothing looks like it's compensating. The absence of persuasion mechanics reads as confidence.
Content gets the room
Generous margins frame the subject the way a wall frames a work. Density is a choice made per-section, never globally.
Editorial, not commercial
Sentence case, plain nouns, no exclamation, no urgency. Labels describe; they don't recruit.
Hierarchy without noise
Weight and gray do the work that color, boxes, and shadows do elsewhere. Fewer signals, each one legible.
Repetition over novelty
One card, one button pair, one divider, reused everywhere. Familiarity within a page is the point.
Quiet motion
140–200ms color and opacity transitions only. No parallax, reveal-on-scroll, or transform easing.
Portability
What travels, and what doesn't
Everything above is subject-neutral. Three things in the reference are domain artifacts and should not be carried into a new context: the media-first card ratio, the commerce-shaped detail line, and the favorite/status affordances. Swap the media well for a text block, the detail line for a date or role, and drop the icon action — the grid, type scale, and tone are untouched.
If the subject is text-led
Body sits at 19–20px and card titles at 27px; tighten row gap from 96 to 64, and let the section heading + hairline pattern carry structure that images carried before. Add one component only: a definition row (label left in micro, value right in body) for dense factual lists. Nothing else needs to change.
